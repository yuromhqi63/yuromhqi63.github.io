01 / The question

Olist connects small sellers to Brazilian marketplaces. Its public dataset links about 100,000 orders (2016–2018) to delivery dates, payments, products and reviews. Almost every customer buys once and never returns. I wanted to find out what in the first shopping experience decides whether a customer comes back, and where the business should act first.

02 / Process

How I built it

  1. 01

    Clean and load into MySQL

    Cleaned 9 raw CSV tables (5-digit zip codes, one review per order, delivered orders only) and loaded them into a normalised MySQL schema running in Docker. From here on, every analysis reads from the database through SQL, never from CSV.

  2. 02

    First-order feature view

    A SQL view with one row per customer's first order: delivery delay bucket, seller–customer distance, cross-state flag, freight ratio, product category, region, order value and review score.

  3. 03

    Explain repurchase

    Logistic regression with odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Model 1 without review score, Model 2 with it, to test whether the review is the channel through which delays hurt.

  4. 04

    Segment customers

    RFM scoring for existing customers, a 90-day repurchase check for first-time buyers, and K-Means (K = 4) on RFM features, each validated on an out-of-time window.

  5. 05

    Add external data

    Joined IBGE GDP per capita by state and Brazil's state boundaries to test income and draw choropleth maps.

  6. 06

    Size the impact

    Ranked regions, distance bands and sellers by how many extra repeat customers each could bring, and mined complaint keywords from reviews.

  • Late delivery is the clearest controllable driver of not coming back, and it works through the bad review it triggers.
  • The Northeast and orders shipped more than 1,500 km are the biggest levers; a small group of sellers drives most delays.
  • Product category matters more than income: bed, bath and furniture buyers return most often.
  • Retention is so low that even the best segment repurchases under 5%, so acquisition cost should be judged on a single order.
